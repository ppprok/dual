## Notes (written by hand after the run)

- **Regenerated after the fixed-width fast path of RUNC (#56)**: `RuncGlobalState` (RUNCM, RUNC,
  OPT) now keeps the rows of matrices with at most 128 columns in one or two 64-bit words. It
  replaces the report made after #45-#49. The machine was more loaded than for that run (load
  average about 9 on 8 logical CPUs, from other users), so absolute times are noisier; the
  comparison with master below was run in the same session, right after this report.
- **RUNCM is now the fastest algorithm almost everywhere** in this grid (all n <= 96), on par with
  RuncFixed (0.5-1.1x of its median time; 240 x 30, p = 0.5: 60 ms vs 65 ms; 50 x 50, p = 0.5:
  59 ms vs 64 ms). RuncFixed only wins at the smallest sizes (tens of microseconds, where the setup
  of the general API dominates).
- **Against master** (same matrices, same session, median time): RUNCM 1.9-5.6x faster (240 x 30,
  p = 0.5: 155 -> 60 ms; 50 x 50, p = 0.5: 200 -> 59 ms; 12 x 96, p = 0.5: 74 -> 18.5 ms), RUNC
  1.7-4.1x, OPT 1.2-3.3x. RUNC and OPT build larger search trees than RUNCM (OPT also removes
  dependent rows in every node, quadratic in the number of rows), so they stay 1.5-3x behind
  RuncFixed. Above 128 columns the general path runs as before (no change measured on 10 x 200 and
  8 x 160 beyond the noise of about 10%).
- **m << n (`wide`):** up to 96 columns RUNCM is now ahead of the AOT1 K/M variants that led there
  (9 x 72, p = 0.2: RUNCM 13 ms, AO1K 21 ms; 12 x 96, p = 0.5: RUNCM 18.5 ms, AO1K 66 ms). Beyond
  128 columns RUNCM runs on the general path, where the AOT1 K/M variants were 1.5-3x ahead of it.
- **Time per cover** (median over completed runs with at least 100 covers): RUNCM 0.15 us,
  RuncFixed 0.19 us, AO1K 0.23 us, RUNC 0.28 us, OPT 0.50 us, PUNC-RC 0.57 us, AO2M 0.58 us,
  FK 10 us (master in the same session: RUNCM 0.45 us, RUNC 0.76 us, OPT 1.13 us). All algorithms
  agree on the number of covers.
